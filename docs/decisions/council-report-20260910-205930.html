Council Report — Whole-Project Review

AI Recruiter Agent · counciled 2026-09-10 · 5 advisors + 5 peer reviews + chairman
The question Judge the AI Recruiter Agent as it stands today — bottlenecks, security threats, improvements, unnecessary work, architecture problems — in plain language. A zero-recurring-cost web app (FastAPI + React + Supabase) where a recruiter describes a hiring need and an AI runs the pipeline (requirement parse → form → distribute → monitor → invite → live AI voice interview → scorecard) up to but not including the human hire/reject decision. Candidate PII under the EU AI Act (recruitment = high-risk) and India's DPDP Act. Built: LLM Router, 5 connectors, 2 agents, Owner auth (Supabase Auth + mandatory MFA), 17-table schema, Owner Console, 74 tests. Not built: distribution, monitoring, notifications, meeting creation, the live voice interview (largest, highest-uncertainty), scoring, decision screen, persistence.
COUNCIL VERDICT

Stop building forward. The riskiest part is unbuilt, unproven, and may not be feasible.

The project has finished the safe, easy portion and left its defining feature — a custom bot that joins a Zoom/Meet call and talks to candidates by voice — entirely unbuilt. Four of five advisors, independently, said the planned way of building it (a Playwright browser bot wired through OS-level virtual audio cables on a free cloud VM) is likely to fail. Nothing downstream can be planned until that question is answered.

Where the council agrees

  • Phase 5 (the live voice interview) is the whole ballgame — and the planned build path is the most fragile one available. Use your own WebRTC room (LiveKit Agents / Pipecat) or Zoom's Meeting SDK, never audio-scraping a meeting.
  • The service-role key in the backend is a live PII hole. The backend bypasses row-level security and re-implements tenant isolation as "remember to filter by owner_id in every handler." One missed filter exposes every candidate's data across every recruiter account.
  • "Zero recurring cost" has become a liability. The quota-pause machinery, the five-model chains, the keep-alive cron, and the shared key all exist to dodge a ~$20/month bill and are actively forcing bad architecture.
  • Nobody has validated the premise. No design partner, no real open role, no evidence that mechanical screening (rather than sourcing or judgment) is the actual bottleneck.
  • The "turn it into a platform" framing is premature. All five peer reviews flagged it — there is no product yet to build a platform around.

Where the council clashes

  • Drop the live interview vs. build it right. One advisor says replace it with asynchronous recorded screening (candidate records answers, you transcribe + score) — same signal, far less risk. Another says keep it but only in a controlled WebRTC room. Resolution: the two-week spike decides; async is the fallback.
  • Is the LLM Router an asset or a liability? One advisor calls it the crown jewel worth extracting as its own product; another calls it a single point of daily failure with no floor. Both are right — the engineering is good and reusable; depending on it as the only inference path for a high-risk system is fragile.
  • How much of the current build is over-engineering. Mandatory MFA, 17 tables, and a three-cloud plan for zero users — premature, or cheap-to-keep and costly-to-retrofit?

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • A human making the final call does not remove the high-risk classification. AI that screens, ranks, or filters who reaches a human is itself high-risk under the EU AI Act. The provider obligations — DPIA, conformity assessment, bias/accuracy testing, technical documentation, logging, post-market monitoring — attach to the operator regardless of architecture. For a solo unpaid builder this is likely the binding constraint on the whole project.
  • A solo builder cannot be the "human oversight" for their own automated system in any meaningful sense.
  • Candidate-side mechanics are entirely absent: standards-compliant consent, the right to an explanation, an appeal path, and the dropout cost of asking nervous candidates to talk to a bot.
  • Nobody has checked that the AI screening signal predicts job performance. If it doesn't, the pipeline just delivers a worse decision faster.

The recommendation

Three things, in order:

  • 1. Two-week timeboxed voice spike, now, before Phases 2–4. Build it in your own WebRTC room (LiveKit or Pipecat). Hard go/no-go at day 14. If no-go, switch to async recorded screening — it reaches a shippable product much sooner.
  • 2. Close the PII hole regardless. Move the backend to per-request, JWT-scoped Supabase connections so row-level security is the live enforcement layer, not a dormant copy of rules you also hand-maintain in Python. This one can't wait.
  • 3. Get a written answer on the EU AI Act question before writing more agent code. Either scope to genuine decision-support with independent human judgment at every gate (and take legal advice on whether that still triggers full obligations), or exclude the EU at launch and serve India only, or accept the compliance program as part of the build. And line up one real recruiter with one real open role as a design partner before Phase 2.

On zero-cost: keep it as a default, not a constraint. Assume a small paid tier is available where free tiers force bad design, and note where you're choosing to stay free. Keep the LLM Router, the schema, and the Owner auth. Don't extract the router as a product yet.

The one thing to do first: Run the two-week voice spike in your own WebRTC room (LiveKit or Pipecat), starting now, ending in a written go/no-go. The schedule, the async fallback, the compliance scope, and whether this is even the right product all depend on that answer — and you don't have it yet.

Full advisor responses
The First Principles Thinker
Almost none of the build touches the real job: less recruiter time spent on mechanical work. The live-voice-interview goal is a framing error. What the recruiter actually needs is a reliable signal about a candidate. A voice bot screen-scraping Google Meet through virtual audio cables is the most fragile, most regulated, most gameable way to get that signal. Asynchronous screening — the candidate records answers to set questions, you transcribe and score them — produces the same signal with a fraction of the technical and regulatory risk. "Zero cost" is the wrong north star: the quota-pause machinery, the keep-alive cron, the five-model chains are all elaborate taxes paid to dodge a ~$20/month bill. A rebuilt v1: skip the live interview entirely — form → distribute → async recorded screening → transcript + scorecard → human decides. Ship that, get one real hire through it, then decide whether a live bot is worth the trouble.
The Expansionist
The LLM Router is the crown jewel and it's buried as plumbing. Extract it as a standalone open-source library plus a hosted "AI reliability router" — provider fallback, quota-aware routing, and graceful degradation are a real, unsolved pain for anyone building on free or cheap LLM tiers. Multilingual Hindi/Marathi voice interviewing is a genuine moat: English-first interview bots can't serve the 500M+ vernacular Indian workforce. The wedge is vernacular-first hiring for Bharat, with Apna / WorkIndia / Vahan as distribution channels or eventual acquirers. Compliance-by-design, done properly, is itself a sellable artifact. Position for this now: instrument every LLM call, routing decision, and scorecard into an event log; namespace the agents so they're pluggable; move the router into its own repo; and treat the transcript store as the eventual product, not a byproduct.
The Executor
Rough timing for one person: Phase 2 (distribution + monitoring) — weeks. Phase 3 (notifications) — days. Phase 4 (meeting creation, Zoom only, skip Meet) — weeks. Phase 5 (live interview) — months. Phase 6 (scoring + decision screen) — weeks. The virtual-audio bot on a free Oracle VM is a rabbit hole: PulseAudio null sinks + Playwright + headless Chrome + Zoom's bot detection + WebRTC timing, all on a 1-OCPU / 1GB ARM instance. Chrome's Web Speech API is undocumented for server-side use and has no SLA. Zoom actively fights bots. Expect 6–8 weeks before you even know if it works. Don't build the media pipeline from scratch — use Zoom's Meeting SDK, or LiveKit Agents / Pipecat, and run the interview in your own WebRTC room where you control the audio. Highest-leverage move right now: a timeboxed two-week Phase 5 voice spike, before Phases 2–4, ending in a hard go/no-go.
The Contrarian
The fatal flaw: the whole edifice rests on abusing free tiers to run a legally high-risk workload. The LLM Router is a single point of daily failure with no floor under it — "auto-resumes at quota reset" is not the human oversight the EU AI Act requires; it's an outage dressed up as a feature. The service-role key living in the app tier is a PII breach waiting to happen: "filter by owner_id in every endpoint" is a promise to never make a mistake across dozens of handlers, and one missing .eq("owner_id") exposes the entire candidate database across tenants. RLS was the actual defense, and it was deliberately bypassed. Use per-request, JWT-scoped database connections and keep RLS live. Meanwhile the project is over-built for its stage: mandatory MFA, 17 tables, a 5-model chain, and a three-cloud deployment for zero users and no persistence. Zero-cost is a liability that is actively forcing bad architecture — the shared key and the quota-pause both vanish the moment you accept a ~$20/month bill.
The Outsider
Picture the actual moment: a nervous candidate who needs the job is talking to a robot that decides whether they advance — in Hindi or Marathi, through a browser speech engine tuned for English. The off-script answers, the accents, the bad connections, the thinking pauses — that is exactly where it breaks, and those are the moments that matter. The whole thing is backwards: the builder has finished the safe, easy 20% and left the risky core unbuilt while already committing to it in the README. The fact that the audio plumbing is described as the "hardest, least-certain" piece is a warning that the product may simply not be feasible as described. "$0/month forever" openly conflicts with the ambition. This is almost certainly a high-risk AI system that needs human-oversight documentation, bias audits, candidate notifications, and a DPIA — a serious compliance load for one person. And hand-reimplementing database protection in every single query works right up until the one time someone forgets.
Peer review highlights
What the five reviews converged on
Strongest response: the Contrarian (3 of 5 votes) for the sharpest current-state critique — the service-role key makes RLS decorative, and quota-pause is not human oversight. The Executor took the other 2 votes for the most constructive answer: build the interview in your own WebRTC room and dissolve the OS-audio problem entirely. Biggest blind spot: the Expansionist, on all 5 reviews — it treats a legally high-risk, zero-user, half-built project as a platform play, and "instrument everything into an event log" adds discoverable evidence to a system with no compliance process behind it. What all five advisors missed (raised only in peer review): • A human making the final decision does not remove the high-risk classification — AI that screens or ranks who reaches a human is itself high-risk, and the provider obligations attach regardless of architecture. • A solo builder cannot be the independent "human oversight" for their own automated system. • Candidate-side consent, right-to-explanation, appeal, and dropout economics are entirely absent. • Nobody has validated that mechanical screening is the recruiter's actual bottleneck, or that the AI screening signal predicts job performance.
